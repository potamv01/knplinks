The midlife ear and balance checklist

A five-minute structured screen for adults aged 40–65 presenting with ear fullness, pressure, autophony, dizziness or unsteadiness. Separates obstructive Eustachian tube dysfunction, patulous Eustachian tube dysfunction and vestibular causes — and flags what needs ENT or emergency assessment first.

A five-minute questionnaire for anyone aged 40–65 with blocked, full or crackling ears, echoing of your own voice, dizziness or unsteadiness. It helps you work out what to describe to your GP, and what needs checking urgently.

    Obstructive ETD 0

    Failure to dilate. Fullness, muffled hearing, popping, worse with colds, altitude and flying. Consider ETDQ-7, tympanometry, treat nasal inflammation first.

    The tube is struggling to open. Ears feel blocked and muffled, and worse with colds or flying.

    Patulous ETD 0

    Abnormally patent tube. Autophony of voice and breathing, relieved lying down or by sniffing. Ask about rapid weight loss, including GLP-1 agonists and bariatric surgery. Decongestants and auto-inflation make it worse.

    The tube is staying open when it should be shut. Your own voice and breathing sound loud inside your head, and lying down helps.

    Vestibular cause 0

    Inner ear or central balance origin rather than middle ear. Sub-type by duration and trigger, then treat with positional manoeuvres or vestibular rehabilitation.

    The dizziness is coming from the balance organ or its brain pathways, rather than from ear pressure.

    Step 5

    When bloods are worth checking

    Bloods do not diagnose Eustachian tube dysfunction and no supplement treats it. Their value is in excluding mimics of unsteadiness and in one specific place with trial evidence: reducing BPPV recurrence in deficient patients. Order on indication, not as a panel.

    Blood tests cannot diagnose an ear tube problem, and no vitamin will fix one. They are useful for ruling out other causes of unsteadiness — and in one situation, for reducing how often positional vertigo comes back.

    TestCheck whenWhat it changesEvidence
    25(OH) vitamin D Recurrent BPPV, especially postmenopausal; housebound or low sun exposure Correcting deficiency reduces BPPV recurrence in deficient patients; supports bone and muscle function RCT / meta-analysis
    B12 and folate Unsteadiness without spinning, wide-based gait, altered sensation, metformin or PPI use, vegan diet Deficiency mimics vestibular unsteadiness and is reversible if caught early Observational / case
    FBC and ferritin Fatigue, breathlessness, heavy perimenopausal bleeding, lightheadedness on standing Anaemia is a common non-vestibular cause of "dizzy" in this age group Observational
    Thyroid function Unexplained fatigue, weight change, palpitations alongside dizziness Overlaps heavily with menopausal symptoms and is easily missed Mimic exclusion
    HbA1c, U&E, calcium Falls, postural symptoms, polypharmacy, rapid or intentional weight loss Postural hypotension, electrolyte and glycaemic causes of unsteadiness Mimic exclusion

    Note on rapid weight loss: sudden loss of weight — including on GLP-1 receptor agonists or after bariatric surgery — is a recognised trigger for patulous Eustachian tube symptoms, through reduction of the Ostmann fat pad that holds the tube closed. This is a mechanical consequence, not a nutritional deficiency, and it usually improves as weight stabilises.
